# Animation Rules

- No stiff character movement.
- Every attack must include anticipation, action, impact, and recovery.
- Camera movement should follow or reveal action rather than shake randomly.
- Use speed ramps only where they improve impact or readability.
- Use motion blur selectively on genuinely fast movement.
- Use environmental reactions such as debris, dust, leaves, particles, and lighting when appropriate.
- Strong hits need clear contact timing, body momentum, and follow-through.
- Running should include foot planting, body lean, arm swing, shoulder movement, and believable acceleration.
- Falling or launched characters should rotate and preserve momentum naturally.
- Shorts must establish a strong visual hook within the first 1–2 seconds.
- Use contrast between quiet moments and explosive action to improve pacing.
- Avoid long static dialogue scenes in Shorts.
- Prefer deliberate close-ups, wide shots, low angles, overhead shots, tracking shots, and orbit shots over arbitrary cuts.
- Build loopable endings where the story naturally supports them.
